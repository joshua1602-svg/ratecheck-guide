How to check your rateable value in 2026

Your property's rateable value is not the same as the amount you pay in business rates. It is a valuation set by the Valuation Office for properties in England and Wales, which local authorities use when working out business rates.

1. Find your official rateable value

Use the official GOV.UK business rates valuation search. Search for the property and check its address, description, valuation and any available valuation details. For the current rating list, focus on the valuation effective from 1 April 2026.

2. Understand the difference between rateable value and rates payable

The rateable value is an assessment of the property's annual rental value at the relevant valuation date. The bill is calculated using the relevant multiplier, with adjustments for any applicable reliefs or other rules. A property with a £30,000 rateable value does not necessarily have a £30,000 annual rates bill.

3. Compare similar premises

For a useful initial sense-check, compare properties with similar use, location, size and valuation basis. Differences in frontage, layout, floor levels, ancillary space and other features can matter. A nearby property's lower rateable value is not, by itself, evidence that yours is wrong.

What if your details appear incorrect?

Check the official record and use the Valuation Office's prescribed process to correct property information or raise a check case. If you disagree with the valuation after the check case, there is a separate challenge procedure with evidence and deadlines.
